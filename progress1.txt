Subject: Project Update: Part 1 Complete - From Zero to Virtual Campus

Hi Team,

I am excited to share that we have officially completed Part 1 of our AR Navigation App.

Today, I successfully imported the 2D map of our college into Unity and have begun building the 3D walls and doors on top of it. Seeing the actual hallway dimensions (39m x 3.5m) come to life in the app is a huge milestone.

To get here, we have tackled some significant technical challenges since starting from scratch. Here is a breakdown of the journey and the specific issues we resolved:

1. CORE AR & LOCALIZATION (The Heavy Lifting)
This was the most complex part of the build. We needed the digital world to align perfectly with the physical college building.

- The "Teleport" Issue: Early on, scanning a QR code would snap the camera directly into the wall anchor.
  * Solution: We implemented a position offset logic (-0.5m distance) that calculates the correct standing position, pushing the user back to a comfortable viewing distance upon scanning.

- The "Rotation Flip" Issue: Scanning a QR code on an opposite wall (e.g., South vs. North) would initially flip the entire world upside down or 180 degrees.
  * Solution: We evolved from a hard-coded "Switch Case" system to a "Smart Alignment" system. The code now dynamically reads the rotation of the reference object in Unity, allowing us to support North, South, East, and West entrances automatically without rewriting code.

- Real-World Scaling: We successfully calculated the Unity-to-Meters scale (1 Unity Unit = 1 Meter), ensuring that our 39m virtual hallway matches the real-world hallway stride-for-stride.

2. DATABASE INTEGRATION
- We moved away from storing data locally in code.
- Firebase Firestore is now fully integrated. The app dynamically fetches QR Anchor data (Coordinates + Direction) from the cloud. This allows us to update anchor positions without rebuilding the whole app.

3. UI & USER EXPERIENCE (UX)
Making the app usable was a separate challenge from making it work.

- The "Infinite List" Problem: As we added more classrooms (1165, 1163, etc.), the buttons ran off the screen.
  * Solution: We rewrote the OnGUI loop to include a dynamic Scroll View that automatically adjusts its height based on the number of rooms in the database.

- Navigation Flow: We refined the menu layout, moving the "Scan QR" button to the top for quick access and resizing elements for better mobile usability.

- Control Logic: We fixed a persistent bug where the "Close/X" button wouldn't hide the scanner canvas due to unassigned inspector slots. The UI now cleanly opens, closes, and auto-hides upon a successful scan.

CURRENT STATUS:
The foundation is rock solid. The scanner works, the database connects, the world aligns itself to any of the 4 cardinal directions, and the UI handles our large list of classrooms smoothly. The 2D map is imported, and the walls are going up.

NEXT STEPS:
Now that the physical structure is in place, the next phase is Population:
1. Placing the specific classroom POIs (Points of Interest) into their exact spots on the new 3D map.
2. Refining the navigation arrow to guide users around the newly built walls.
